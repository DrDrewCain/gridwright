# Commercial licensing

gridwright is licensed under the **GNU Affero General Public License v3.0**
([LICENSE](LICENSE)). It is genuinely open source: use it, read it, modify it,
redistribute it, run it in production, all without asking anyone.

The AGPL asks one thing in return. If you modify gridwright and make it
available to others over a network, you have to publish your modified source
under the AGPL as well. That obligation reaches the whole work it is combined
with, not just the files you changed.

## Who this affects

**Nobody who is doing the thing this was built for.** Research, teaching,
national laboratories, NGOs, regulators, grid operators publishing their own
methodology, anyone modelling a decarbonisation pathway and showing their
working — the AGPL costs you nothing, because you were going to publish anyway.

**Companies who need to keep their stack closed.** If gridwright is embedded in
a commercial product, or offered as a hosted service, and you do not want to
release the surrounding source, the AGPL does not permit that. A commercial
licence does.

That is the entire distinction: not how large you are, but whether you can meet
the reciprocity the AGPL asks for.

## Getting a commercial licence

A commercial licence removes the copyleft obligation and can include support and
priority on features. It is granted case by case by the author.

Open an issue at <https://github.com/DrDrewCain/gridwright/issues> titled
`commercial licence` describing the intended use, and it will be picked up from
there.

## Why this arrangement

Energy system models inform decisions about where public infrastructure gets
built. Modelling done in public should stay in public, which is what the AGPL
enforces. Modelling done inside a company that will not show its working is a
different kind of use, and it is reasonable for that to fund the work.

Dual licensing is a well travelled way to hold both of those at once: it is how
MySQL, Qt and Cal.com are licensed, among many others. The code is the same
either way, and nothing is held back from the open version.
